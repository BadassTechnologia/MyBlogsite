NVIDIA shares rose 31.48 percent on November 23, 2026, and still ended that session more than 18 percent below their November 2 close. That combination is possible because the rally began after a steep decline. It also explains why a headline about the comeback and a headline about the month's losses can both describe the same trading period accurately.

Through the November 24 quote available at 1:41 p.m. Eastern, NVIDIA had dropped from $202.06 at the first US trading close of the month to $153.83, an indicative decline of about 23.9 percent. The November 24 value is an intraday quote, so the most recent verified closing-price endpoint in this analysis is November 23 at $165.05. The move from November 2 to that close was down about 18.3 percent. The distinction matters in a stock that can move materially in a single session.

## The first decline preceded the CEO transition

The stock had already moved down before NVIDIA announced changes in leadership. From November 2 to November 5 it lost $20.46 per share, or about 10.1 percent. It rebounded to $186.64 on November 6 and remained roughly 7.6 percent below the starting close. That chronology puts a limit on explanations that attribute all November losses to events disclosed later in the month.

NVIDIA's board released a statement on November 8 announcing an independent investigation. By November 9, the next trading session, the shares closed at $178.67. On November 12 the company said Jensen Huang had resigned as president, CEO, and a director with immediate effect, and Colette Kress became interim CEO. The close that day was $166.19, down 4.34 percent from the previous session. The full trading range was wide, reflecting how different the prices paid during that session could be from the closing observation.

It is reasonable to identify the transition as an event that introduced leadership uncertainty. A founder-led company can be valued partly on expectations about strategy, relationships, and continuity, so a sudden change in responsibility gives investors something new to assess. That interpretation does not require making claims about matters under investigation. The board's statements establish the announcement and sequence of leadership changes. Stock quotes establish what people paid at different points. Neither tells us the motive of every participant.

## Why strong earnings failed to halt the decline

On November 17, NVIDIA reported $115.8 billion of revenue for fiscal 2027's third quarter, up 20 percent from the prior quarter and 103 percent from a year earlier. Data Center revenue reached $107.6 billion, approximately 93 percent of total revenue in the quarter. The company reported GAAP diluted earnings per share of $2.70 and non-GAAP diluted earnings per share of $2.68. Those use different accounting definitions, so compare each with estimates or past results on the same basis.

A good earnings result answers a question about business performance through a specific date. NVIDIA's reported quarter ended on October 25. The equity price on November 17 also reflected expectations about future quarters, the risk associated with those expectations, and alternatives in the market. Those can change in different directions. If investors anticipate stronger earnings but simultaneously assign a lower valuation to those earnings, a share price can decline even as revenue and earnings grow.

The numbers illustrate that difference without proving any one explanation. The stock closed at $155.46 on November 17 and $150.96 on November 18. By November 20 it had fallen to $125.53. That was about 19.3 percent below its earnings-day close. The decline continued despite the revenue growth just reported. To determine whether the earnings release itself changed expectations, look for changes in comparable consensus estimates, the reaction to management's outlook, and other news during those same hours. A multi-day chart alone cannot isolate the release's impact.

The Data Center share provides a useful lens for interpreting the reaction. A larger concentration in one reporting category means assumptions about demand in that category matter to the consolidated outlook. An analyst looking forward would examine customer investment plans, delivery timing, margins, and constraints identified in filings. An analyst describing the price move should keep those questions distinct from the audited or reported numbers already in the earnings release.

## November 20 to 23 shows how rebound math works

NVIDIA closed at $125.53 on Friday, November 20. On Monday, November 23, NVIDIA announced that Huang was returning as president, CEO, and a member of the board. The shares opened at $167.04, reached an intraday high of $171.50, and closed at $165.05. The close-to-close gain was $39.52 per share, or 31.48 percent. The open above the previous close shows that much of the difference was already reflected when regular trading resumed, although the price continued to move during the day.

A rebound around a leadership announcement is consistent with investors revising how they view continuity. Monday's move also came during a broader rebound in US stocks, especially technology shares. The Associated Press reported a gain of about 6.5 percent in the Nasdaq Composite on November 23. NVIDIA's gain was much larger on that day. An index comparison gives context, but the difference between the two returns is not a clean estimate of the appointment's effect. The index includes NVIDIA, and market and sector moves can have different effects on individual shares.

The percentage arithmetic shows why the rally did not erase the drawdown. From November 2 to November 20, the stock fell from $202.06 to $125.53, about 37.9 percent. To return to $202.06 from $125.53 would require a gain of about 61.0 percent, because the gain would start from a smaller denominator. The actual gain to $165.05 recovered $39.52 of the earlier $76.53 per-share decline, or just over half the dollar gap. These are three different descriptions of the same path.

As a quick check, divide the final price by the starting price and subtract one. Using Friday's close as the starting point gives the Monday rally of 31.48 percent. Using November 2 as the starting point gives a November 23 price return of about minus 18.3 percent. Avoid adding the largest down and up days together. Multiplying daily growth factors, or calculating the return from the two endpoints directly, gives the correct result.

## What did November 24 add to the story?

At 1:41 p.m. Eastern on November 24, the stock was quoted at $153.83, below the previous day's $165.05 close. That was an $11.22 decline, about 6.8 percent. It showed that buyers had not maintained all of Monday's gains into the following afternoon. A partial-session quote can change before the bell, so it should be presented next to its timestamp whenever it is used to describe a price or return.

One plausible interpretation is that removing some leadership uncertainty improved sentiment on Monday while leaving other questions about valuation and the business outlook open. Another possibility is that the stock was following wider risk appetite as technology shares repriced. Both can be considered alongside actual index and peer movements. A claim that one was the dominant cause would need more evidence than the direction of the next quote.

Trading volume can help describe the intensity of a move. The historical series shows heavy trading on November 20 and 23 compared with the start of the month. High volume confirms substantial turnover. It does not reveal, by itself, whether long-only funds, hedgers, retail traders, or short sellers accounted for a particular part of the price move. To identify those mechanisms, use evidence that can actually observe them, or describe them as possibilities.

## How to update this analysis after the next close

Start with the official disclosure timeline. Check NVIDIA's investor relations page for new board announcements and financial releases. Record the publication time and the first regular session affected. Combine each event with a matching price observation and, if a broad market explanation is relevant, an index reference taken at the same point. Make a note if the stock is moving after hours and keep that quote separate from the regular close.

Next, decide what comparison you want to make. For performance since the first November trading close, keep $202.06 as the baseline and replace only the end price with a verified later close. For a conventional month-to-date comparison, use the last October close and label the change in methodology. When comparing with the Nasdaq Composite or a semiconductor index, apply the same first and last session to both. The comparison becomes misleading if the stock is measured at a live quote and the index at yesterday's close.

Finally, separate what changed in the business from what changed in its valuation. Revenue, earnings, and the mix of sales can be traced to releases or filings. Expectations depend on the estimates used and when they were recorded. A company's price reaction is public, but assigning a motive requires a reasoned interpretation. Summarizing all three parts helps explain why the stock could post a large recovery in one session and still show a substantial loss over the broader window.

## Which price and volume figures can you compare?

A useful check is to calculate the dollar move and percentage move side by side for the same pair of observations. Between the November 17 and November 20 closes, the difference was $29.93 per share. Between November 20 and 23 the change was $39.52 in the other direction. The underlying prices establish that the Monday rebound more than recovered the post-earnings dollar loss, although it did far less to recover the total decline from the beginning of November. You can answer both questions from the same chart once you label the baseline.

If you add volume, keep it in shares and write whether it refers to a completed session. A turnover comparison using dollar volume would need an appropriate price measure over the session. That choice matters on a day when the stock's low and high are far apart. A single closing quote multiplied by all shares traded gives an approximation rather than the value of every executed trade.

## What the November swings show

The first November decline began before NVIDIA's CEO transition was announced. A strong earnings print arrived during the selloff, and the stock recovered sharply when Huang returned. Even after that rebound, the November 23 close remained below the first session's close, and the November 24 intraday observation pointed to a partial reversal. The timeline puts those changes in order without treating any of the news events as a complete explanation for the month's performance.
