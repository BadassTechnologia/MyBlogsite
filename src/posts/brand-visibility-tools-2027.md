When people see your brand more often, you might notice more branded searches, more impressions for your website, or more mentions in articles and conversations. Those changes can occur independently. A successful podcast appearance can get people talking before they click through to your site. An SEO campaign can increase impressions for a product guide without changing how many people search for your company by name. Choose the tool that measures the change you need to explain.

Start with the native search reports for any website you control, then add a mention monitor if your team needs to see discussion outside your own pages. A competitor research subscription makes more sense when you have a regular use for estimated traffic, competitive keywords, or links. This guide compares ten options across those jobs and gives you a way to test coverage before you commit.

## 1. Google Search Console

[Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about) is the best first step for measuring how often your verified website appears in Google organic search and how many clicks it receives. Create a filter for your brand and known name variants, then compare impressions and clicks with the previous comparable period. Also inspect the pages Google shows for those searches. More impressions are useful context when you can see whether people are landing on a homepage, a product guide, or a page intended for existing users.

A branded query filter only tells you about the searches and site impressions represented in the report. It can omit some low-volume queries, and other people can discuss your brand without ever encountering your site in search. Use a separate mention source to observe that behavior. If your name is also a common word, check a sample of the actual query strings before calling them brand demand.

**Pricing**. The service is free for verified properties.

## 2. Google Trends

[Google Trends](https://trends.google.com/trends/) helps you see relative interest in search terms or topics over time. For example, compare your company and a competitor in the same geographic area and date range to see whether a launch changed the shape of interest. Choose a consistent search type and period before reusing the result in reports. Changing the frame can change the values on the same graph, because the values are normalized to the comparison.

Use a topic where it maps cleanly to your brand. Use a term when the exact phrase matters. With small brands or narrowly defined regions, the graph may have too little data to give you a useful pattern. Extend the observation period or look at a broader region where that change is appropriate to your business question.

**Pricing**. The public service is free.

[Image suggestion: Screenshot of the official Google Trends landing page.]

## 3. Bing Webmaster Tools

[Bing Webmaster Tools](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/about) helps you observe how a verified property performs on Bing. Set it up when your team wants visibility across more than one search engine and needs to distinguish indexing issues from traffic changes. A dashboard for a brand campaign can show Google and Bing separately, using comparable windows and keeping each service's reporting definitions visible.

Use this tool when a competitor estimate would be a poor substitute for data about your own domain. Before drawing a conclusion from a difference between engines, check the relative volume and which landing pages account for the change. A large percentage change from a small base can be less consequential than a small change on a channel where many more people find you.

**Pricing**. The verified-site service is free.

[Image suggestion: Screenshot of Microsoft's official Bing Webmaster Tools landing page.]

## 4. Ahrefs

[Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/pricing) fits teams that need competitive search and backlink research in addition to their first-party search data. A content team might inspect which sites link to a competitor's reference guide and what kinds of queries the guide appears for. That can suggest a format or distribution channel worth evaluating. Compare results for domains with similar geography and goals, then validate any assumption about your own site's traffic in your native analytics.

Decide which reports you need before selecting a plan. The public pricing comparison distinguishes plans, users, limits, and optional products. A team that needs frequent exports or multiple users may need a different plan than a solo researcher doing occasional checks. Treat third-party keyword and traffic figures as directional estimates.

**Pricing**. Ahrefs lists a Starter option. The monthly comparison shows Lite at $129 per month. Check the current Starter allowance and checkout price for the region and account you would use.

[Image suggestion: Screenshot of the official Ahrefs landing page.]

## 5. Semrush

[Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/pricing/seo-ai-search/) suits teams monitoring a set of target keywords, competitors, and locations over time. Define the tracked set before a campaign starts. That gives you a consistent trend even as individual keywords become more or less popular. If you also want a report about visibility in AI-generated answers, verify which surfaces, reports, and projects are included in the tier you are considering.

For a fair test, use one country and device configuration, plus a list of known competitors. Test whether teammates can explain why the visibility result moved. The ability to drill into a useful set of underlying queries is often more valuable than a headline score that nobody on the marketing team can reproduce.

**Pricing**. The SEO tier is listed at $139 per month. The annual term displays the equivalent of $117.33 per month. Separate tiers add features, so compare coverage for the specific report you need.

[Image suggestion: Screenshot of the official Semrush landing page.]

## 6. Brand24

[Brand24](https://brand24.com/prices/) is intended for teams that need to monitor brand mentions and report on the sources a project covers. A useful pilot starts with a brand name, a product name, and a few exclusions for unrelated topics. Ask a teammate to review a sample of results. That can reveal whether the volume comes from distinct discussions, repeated links, or unrelated uses of the brand name.

If you plan to report sentiment, sample the results in the languages that matter to you. A complaint may quote a positive phrase sarcastically, and an automated label can miss that context. Preserve a selection of source links so readers can review the tone and substance behind a graph.

**Pricing**. The first listed plan is $249 month to month or $199 per month equivalent on annual billing. Check keyword, mention, and update allowances before committing to either term.

[Image suggestion: Screenshot of the official Brand24 landing page.]

## 7. Mention

[Mention](https://mention.com/en/pricing/) helps communications teams organize alerts for terms and review the results. It is useful when handoffs matter as much as detection. For a launch or an incident, first decide who reviews an incoming result and what qualifies for escalation. Then test whether the plan you are considering can filter and share results in a way that matches that process.

Before booking a demo, make a list of five or ten public posts or articles you would expect a relevant monitor to surface. Ask about source access, search history, alert thresholds, and exports. The list gives you a concrete basis for comparing coverage with your current method.

**Pricing**. A company plan and demo are listed on the official page. A reliable numeric starting price was unavailable from the public page checked.

[Image suggestion: Screenshot of the official Mention landing page.]

## 8. Google Alerts

[Google Alerts](https://www.google.com/alerts) is a simple starting point for getting emails about new web results for a phrase. Try an exact brand name and a product name as separate alerts. As mentions arrive, build a short list of common unrelated terms to exclude. This is useful for a founder or communications lead who wants a lightweight way to notice coverage without checking a dashboard throughout the day.

Treat missing alerts carefully. Web discovery and alert inclusion are different from a guarantee that every source will be covered. When coverage is a priority, test the alerts against a known announcement and compare the results with a manual search.

**Pricing**. The alert service is free.

[Image suggestion: Screenshot of the official Google Alerts landing page.]

## 9. Talkwalker Alerts

[Talkwalker Alerts](https://www.talkwalker.com/alerts) is a free keyword alert option for monitoring covered online sources. It works well as a second feed alongside an existing alert because you can run the same phrase through both tools and compare the overlap. For example, track the company name, a spokesperson's public professional name, and a campaign phrase as distinct queries when each has a clear relevance to your work.

A useful weekly review records which items appeared in both feeds, which were unique, and which were irrelevant. If most unmatched results are duplicates or off-topic stories, adding more alerts may increase review time without increasing meaningful coverage.

**Pricing**. The public alerts service is free. It is separate from paid products with additional reporting and monitoring capabilities.

[Image suggestion: Screenshot of the official Talkwalker Alerts landing page.]

## 10. Similarweb

[Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/packages/web/) is most useful when you want a directional comparison of how websites attract visits. A marketing team could compare a competitor's estimated acquisition mix before and after a campaign or investigate whether several companies in a category show the same seasonal pattern. Compare similar businesses and avoid reading a point estimate as a count of the competitor's actual visitors.

Check which geographies, historical periods, and channel views your team can access. For small sites or narrow markets, a coverage gap may make a comparison unsuitable. Use first-party analytics for your own results and keep modelled competitor figures labeled as estimates in any shared report.

**Pricing**. Web Intelligence packages are listed on the official site. Confirm a numeric quote and the exact coverage for the selected package before budgeting.

## Set up a visibility report you can check

Pick a single decision the report needs to support. If the question is whether demand for your brand changed, compare the same branded search queries and the same time period. If the question is whether a campaign appeared in publications or conversations, collect the unique relevant mentions and samples of the actual coverage. If the question is how much a competitor grew, show the modelled estimate and name the independent facts that would help verify it.

Run a two-week pilot before expanding the stack. Track one campaign, a small set of query variants, and one known article or announcement. Ask which metric changed, whether the underlying evidence matches it, and whether your team could act on what it learned. That gives you a basis for choosing a paid tool and an equally good basis for relying on native reporting where it already answers the question.

A final budget check is to estimate review time before adding another feed. If two services surface largely the same links, adding the second may make sense only for the sources the first misses. Put those sources in the pilot brief so the renewal decision can be based on actual coverage.
